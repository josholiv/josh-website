Recently, I [left my position as lab manager](https://josholivier.com/posts/farewell-fnim-lab/) in the University of Texas at Dallas Functional Neuroimaging of Memory lab to prepare to start my PhD at the University of California, Riverside this upcoming fall. I'm stoked to start my PhD and continue to work on memory and brain research in a new lab. I'm also very excited to start a new chapter of my life in California, a state I've never lived in before. Fun fact about me—I'm a chronic mover, and I've lived in every time zone in the contiguous United States (including Arizona, which doesn't observe Daylight Saving Time), except the Pacific Time Zone. So, naturally, I'm excited to check a new time zone off the bucket list. 

As it turns out, starting school in California means moving to California. Because this was a big move, my partner Ivana and I decided to make a big trip out of it with our dog, Oliver. Driving all the way from Dallas to Riverside can take just under 24 hours of total drive time, but we wanted to take it relatively slow. We also wanted to stop at as many national parks as we could, because there are several parks along the route. We stretched the trip into five days and briefly stopped at six national parks: [Carlsbad Caverns](https://www.nps.gov/cave/index.htm), [Guadalupe Mountains](https://www.nps.gov/gumo/index.htm), [White Sands](https://www.nps.gov/whsa/index.htm), [Saguaro](https://www.nps.gov/sagu/index.htm), [Grand Canyon](https://www.nps.gov/grca/index.htm), and [Joshua Tree](https://www.nps.gov/jotr/index.htm). 

## Day 1: Driving to New Mexico

We spent the first day of our trip just driving for about eight hours from Dallas to Carlsbad, New Mexico. Carlsbad was a critical stopping point due to its proximity to the first three national parks we would visit (it's also close to Big Bend, depending on your definition of "close", but we skipped that one). Stopping for the night in Carlsbad set us up nicely to visit Carlsbad Caverns, Guadalupe Mountains, and White Sands national parks all on day two, while still making some decent progress toward our final destination.

## Day 2: Carlsbad Caverns, Guadalupe Mountains, and White Sands

Day 2 was the core of our national park excursion, as we visited three parks in one day. This was an incredible experience, though it goes without saying that we didn't have much time to spend in each park, and therefore were unable to enjoy each to its fullest. Each one of these parks would be worth a dedicated trip. 

Since we started in Carlsbad, Carlsbad Caverns was our first stop of the day. Here, you have the option to hike to the natural cave entrance or just take an elevator directly down into the cavern. We chose to take the elevator because we were short on time. 

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/carlsbad-caverns.jpg" alt="Oliver and me outside the Carlsbad Caverns National Park visitor center" class="blog-body-pic">

The elevator takes you to the Big Room trail, which takes you to the Big Room. Once you get to the Big Room, it's clear why this is a national park. It's huge, and absolutely breathtaking. You could easily spend hours hiking the Big Room trail (with plenty of time to stop and take in all the neat rock formations), but we had to speedrun the trail in order to make it to the next two parks before sunset. Despite spending a relatively short amount of time inside the cavern, this park became an instant favorite for me. 

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/carlsbad-caverns2.jpg" alt="Inside Carlsbad Caverns" class="blog-body-pic">

Another thing to note about Carlsbad Caverns is that it's surprisingly dog-friendly, relative to many other parks. Dogs aren't allowed inside the cavern, but the visitor center provides kennels where you can leave your dog for a small fee while you explore. We personally didn't use the kennels, though, so Ivana and I ended up taking turns, with one of us watching Oliver while the other was inside the cavern. This worked mostly fine, but we couldn't communicate with each other whenever one of us was inside, as there is—understandably—no cell service down there. Oliver had fun walking around outside the visitor center. The pavement was hot, so we had him wear his little shoes. 

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/oliver-drinking-water.jpg" alt="Oliver drinking water" class="blog-body-pic">

After Carlsbad Caverns, we drove about 30 minutes back into Texas to stop by Guadalupe Mountains National Park. There are only a few limited activities you can do with a dog at Guadalupe Mountains, but that was fine, as we weren't planning to stay long. We just walked a short bit of a dog-friendly trail near the visitor center and took some pictures. 

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/guadalupe-mountains.jpg" alt="Ivana and Oliver at Guadalupe Mountains National Park" class="blog-body-pic">

This park looks like it would be awesome for hiking and camping, and the view of the mountains from the visitor center was gorgeous. We might need to plan a return trip at some point. 

After Guadalupe Mountains, we drove for three hours through El Paso to get to White Sands National Park. White Sands is really cool, and it was the most dog-friendly park of our entire trip—they allow you to go anywhere with a dog as long as they are on a leash. Oliver had a blast running around in the sand. 

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/white-sands.jpg" alt="Oliver and me at White Sands National Park" class="blog-body-pic">

I couldn't help but compare White Sands to the beautiful Lençóis Maranhenses National Park in Brazil, which [we visited last year](https://josholivier.com/posts/trip-to-lençóis-maranhenses-national-park/). I think Lençóis takes the cake as far as overall spectacle and beauty, but it was neat to drive through the sand dunes ourselves at White Sands after being driven around by tour guides at Lençóis (the driving path is much tamer at White Sands, though). It was also awesome to have Oliver with us this time. 

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/white-sands2.jpg" alt="Oliver walking on a sand dune" class="blog-body-pic">

## Day 3: Saguaro 

After White Sands, we stayed the night in Las Cruces, New Mexico. The next day we drove four hours straight to Saguaro National Park in Tucson, Arizona. The easiest way to see most of Saguaro is to take the driving loop, and since we were on a road trip, that was perfect for us. 

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/saguaro-national-park.jpg" alt="Oliver and me at Saguaro National Park" class="blog-body-pic">

At this point, Oliver had become a pro at visiting national parks, and I joked that we should get him a Boy Scout vest with a patch for each park he's visited. This didn't apply to our trip, but it turns out that some national parks actually host something called the [B.A.R.K. Ranger](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/pets/be-a-bark-ranger.htm) program for dogs, where you and your dog take a pledge to follow some guidelines:

> - **B**ag your pet’s waste
> - **A**lways leash your pet
> - **R**espect wildlife
> - **K**now where you can go

Oliver enjoyed sticking his head out of the window for the entire drive through Saguaro, apparently taking in the unique landscape. We stopped a couple of times to take some photos by the side of the road. This park was convenient to visit, and we had a blast. 

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/saguaro-national-park2.jpg" alt="The many cacti at Saguaro National Park" class="blog-body-pic">

I'd love to come back here someday with a bike. Tucson is considered one of the [most bike-friendly cities in the United States](https://www.nps.gov/sagu/planyourvisit/bicycling-at-saguaro-national-park.htm), and it's clear why—they have tons of bike infrastructure (bike lanes everywhere!), a variety of terrain, and incredible views. The 8-mile paved driving loop is open to cyclists and looks like a great time, as long as you don't run into a cactus. 

## Day 4: Grand Canyon

The Grand Canyon was a last-minute addition to our trip, and I'm glad we added it, because it was well worth the few hours of extra driving time. We left Tucson on the morning of day four and drove five hours north to the Grand Canyon Village. We were a little tired of driving by that point, so we took our time. We arrived just before sunset, which wasn't ideal for our tight schedule but made for an incredible view. The Grand Canyon is just so gorgeous, and pictures don't do justice to how massive and majestic it is. 

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/grand-canyon.jpg" alt="Oliver and me at the Grand Canyon" class="blog-body-pic">

We only spent about 10 minutes taking in the view before it started getting dark, so we went back to our hotel for the night. Speaking of hotels, a critical aspect of this trip was finding dog-friendly hotels. In fact, the entire point of doing a road trip to California was to avoid flying in a plane with Oliver. As a first-time dog owner, I expected the logistics of finding hotels to be a nightmare, but it was surprisingly easy. Many hotels, including large chains, allow dogs, oftentimes for a modest added fee. Most of the hotels we stayed at even had dog-specific amenities, and Oliver did a great job as a first-time traveler. 

Speaking of getting dark, the Grand Canyon is an [International Dark Sky Park](https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/nature/night-skies.htm), which means the sky gets *really* dark. I took this rare moment away from light pollution to try my hand at some astrophotography. It's nothing crazy, but I got a few decent shots of the Milky Way. 

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/grand-canyon-night-sky.jpg" alt="The Milky Way as seen from Grand Canyon National Park at night" class="blog-body-pic">

## Day 5: Grand Canyon (again) and Joshua Tree

At this point in our trip, we reached a crossroads. We were on a relatively tight, though self-imposed, time schedule to arrive in California, but had only spent a few minutes at the Grand Canyon during the day. We decided to call an audible here and return to the Grand Canyon again in the morning. Luckily, the park entrance ticket lasts for a week, so we could just drive right back in. This allowed us to spend some more time staring into the abyss. Even Oliver seemed interested in looking down into the canyon. I wonder if it was because he was impressed with the view, or just saw a squirrel or something down there, or if he was wondering why we kept taking him to all these weird places.  

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/grand-canyon2.jpg" alt="Oliver and me at the Grand Canyon" class="blog-body-pic">

After seeing the canyon at sunset, it was cool to see how different it looked under the morning sun. Perhaps more so than any of the other parks we'd visited by this point, the Grand Canyon would be well worth returning for a dedicated trip. I'd love to return at some point with more time to do some hiking and other activities. 

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/grand-canyon3.jpg" alt="The Grand Canyon" class="blog-body-pic">

After the Grand Canyon, we just had one more park on the itinerary. We drove for about six hours to arrive at Joshua Tree National Park. Because of rest stops and spending some extra time at the Grand Canyon in the morning, we arrived at Joshua Tree in the late afternoon, but we managed to make it before sunset. 

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/joshua-tree.jpg" alt="The entrance sign at Joshua Tree National Park" class="blog-body-pic">

Joshua Tree was cool, but the Grand Canyon is a tough act to follow. We were also, admittedly, a little tired of driving (and stopping at national parks) at this point in the trip, so we didn't stay long. I plan to come back and check it out more thoroughly sometime soon.

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/joshua-tree2.jpg" alt="Some Joshua trees" class="blog-body-pic">

After a few Google searches on the way to the park, we learned that Joshua trees allegedly got their name because 19th-century Mormon settlers [believed the trees looked like the biblical prophet Joshua](https://www.nps.gov/jotr/learn/nature/jtrees.htm) reaching his arms out. Personally, I don't see how they came to this conclusion (and I don't know how they knew what Joshua looked like!). But Joshua Tree is a pretty cool name, in my completely impartial and unbiased opinion. 

## Conclusion

<img src="/images/blog/post-best-road-trip/grand-canyon-oliver.jpg" alt="A close-up of Oliver at the Grand Canyon" class="blog-body-pic">

In another completely impartial and unbiased opinion, this road trip was one of the greatest road trips of all time! We had a blast, and Oliver was a great sport for the entire trip. He ended up sleeping in the car for about 90% of the time on the road, which really made driving easy. Visiting the national parks provided us with much-needed respite from the relentless monotony of the road. I'm very happy with how dog-friendly the parks, hotels, and the overall trip ended up being, even though our activities were relatively limited in some cases. I hope to take Oliver to more national parks in the future!
